Standard SUPERWOOD Lap Shear Test Design

Methodology, Mechanics & Interactive Dimensioning for High-Strength Adhesive on SUPERWOOD Substrates

1. Calculating Specimen Dimensions for SUPERWOOD

To ensure the glue line shears cleanly before the SUPERWOOD substrate suffers catastrophic tensile failure, the shear force required to break the adhesive ($\mathbf{F_{\text{glue}}}$) must induce a total stress in the SUPERWOOD (tension + eccentricity bending) that stays safely below SUPERWOOD's 150 MPa Modulus of Rupture (MOR).

We calculate this balancing act using the maximum normal stress equation for single-overlap lap joints under axial tension:

$$\sigma_{\text{wood}} = \frac{4 \cdot F_{\text{glue}}}{w \cdot t}$$

Where $w$ is specimen width, $t$ is substrate thickness, and adhesive failure load $F_{\text{glue}} = \tau_{\text{glue}} \cdot w \cdot L$.

To maintain $\sigma_{\text{wood}}$ safely below 150 MPa while generating sufficient force to shear the 15 MPa adhesive, use these engineered dimensions:

Wood Thickness ($t$)

6.0 mm

Provides structural section stiffness to resist out-of-plane peel bending.

Specimen Width ($w$)

20.0 mm

Optimal for standard 25 mm Instron mechanical wedge grips and edge defect mitigation.

Overlap Length ($L$)

5.0 mm

Short overlap length controls the total shear area ($100\text{ mm}^2$) to cap ultimate failure force.

Piece Length & Total Joint

100 mm / 195 mm

Standard 100 mm coupon length per piece yields a 195 mm total assembly length.

SUPERWOOD Math Check Verification:
  • Glue Failure Load ($F_{\text{glue}}$): $15\text{ MPa} \times (20\text{ mm} \times 5\text{ mm}) = \mathbf{1,500\text{ N (1.5 kN)}}$.
  • Peak SUPERWOOD Tensile Stress ($\sigma_{\text{wood}}$): $\frac{4 \times 1500\text{ N}}{20\text{ mm} \times 6\text{ mm}} = \mathbf{50.0\text{ MPa}}$.
  • Result: The adhesive shears cleanly at $1.5\text{ kN}$, while the SUPERWOOD experiences only $50.0\text{ MPa}$ of peak stress—safely below its $150\text{ MPa}$ ultimate MOR threshold.

2. Preparing the Instron Machine Setup

Because single-overlap coupons have offset centerline axes, direct mounting in tension grips causes severe diagonal twisting under load, generating out-of-plane peel stress that fractures wood prematurely.

To eliminate eccentricity, insert 6 mm thick shims (spacer blocks) in the upper and lower grips. Using scrap pieces of your 6 mm Superwood guarantees the shims will not compress under wedge clamping pressures ($E \approx 10\text{ GPa}$).

Step 1: Bond Specimens under Perpendicular Pressure Maintain strict 5.0 mm overlap alignment

Apply adhesive evenly over the $20\text{ mm} \times 5\text{ mm}$ overlap. Secure in an alignment jig under specified curing pressure until full polymerization.

Step 2: Prepare Alignment Shims Must equal substrate thickness ($t = 6\text{ mm}$)

Cut two $20\text{ mm} \times 40\text{ mm}$ pieces from scrap Superwood. These shims offset the grip jaws by exactly 6 mm, centering the bondline along the loading axis.

Step 3: Load Upper Grip & Shim

Place top end of specimen into upper Instron grip. Insert a 6 mm shim on the opposite jaw face. Tighten jaw to secure specimen in a plumb vertical orientation.

Step 4: Zero Load Cell (Tare Weight)

Perform load cell zeroing in Instron Bluehill software to eliminate deadweight tare force of upper specimen grip hardware.

Step 5: Load Lower Grip & Shim

Insert lower specimen end into bottom grip with second 6 mm shim on the opposite jaw face. Verify alignment with a bubble level before locking jaws.

3. Executing the Test Protocol

  1. Quasi-Static Displacement Rate: Set crosshead speed to 1.30 mm/min ($0.05\text{ in/min}$) per ASTM D1002 standard. This quasi-static velocity isolates pure shear behavior without dynamic rate-hardening shocks.
  2. Data Acquisition Rate: Sample Force (N) and Crosshead Extension (mm) at a minimum frequency of 50 Hz.
  3. Shear Strength Calculation: Compute ultimate apparent shear strength ($\tau_{\text{app}}$) by dividing peak load ($F_{\text{peak}}$) by measured bond area ($A = w \cdot L$): $$\tau_{\text{app}} = \frac{F_{\text{peak}}}{w \cdot L}$$
Failure Mode Classification (Post-Test Inspection):
Cohesive Wood Failure
Substrate wood fibers tear out before adhesive fails. Indicates adhesive bond strength exceeds substrate shear capability.
Adhesive (Interfacial) Failure
Clean detachment at the wood-adhesive interface. Points to inadequate surface preparation or contamination.
Cohesive Adhesive Failure
Adhesive splits down its center, leaving residue on both wood coupons. Represents true cohesive shear strength of the polymer.

4. Design Logic & Analytical Mechanics

Single lap joints experience complex combination stresses: axial shear $\tau_{xy}$, transverse peel $\sigma_y$, and eccentric bending moments $M_0$. This section details the mathematical cancellation proof explaining our specimen dimension choices.

The Width Cancellation Theorem

It is often assumed that reducing specimen width ($w$) allows increasing overlap length ($L$). However, mathematically $w$ cancels out entirely from substrate normal stress!

Breakdown of equations:

  • Glue shear failure force: $F = \tau_{\text{glue}} \cdot w \cdot L$
  • Peak substrate tensile bending stress: $\sigma_{\text{wood}} = \frac{4 \cdot F}{w \cdot t}$

Substituting force $F$ into stress equation:

$$\sigma_{\text{wood}} = \frac{4 \cdot (\tau_{\text{glue}} \cdot w \cdot L)}{w \cdot t} \implies \sigma_{\text{wood}} = \frac{4 \cdot \tau_{\text{glue}} \cdot L}{t}$$

Key Engineering Insight: Substrate bending stress depends solely on the ratio of Overlap Length to Substrate Thickness ($\frac{L}{t}$). Specimen width ($w$) only scales the total machine force required, not internal stress!

Why 20.0 mm Width is Optimal

  • Edge Effect Isolation: Narrower coupons ($< 10\text{ mm}$) amplify edge defects and glue squeeze-out fillet artifacts, causing data scatter. 20 mm yields a uniform center stress field.
  • Load Cell Optimization: A $20\text{ mm} \times 5\text{ mm}$ overlap at $15\text{ MPa}$ shear yields $1.5\text{ kN}$ load—operating in the sweet spot ($30\%$) of standard $5.0\text{ kN}$ Instron load cells.
