Standard SUPERWOOD Lap Shear Test Design

Adhesives testing for SUPERWOOD

1. Calculating Specimen Dimensions for SUPERWOOD

To ensure the glue line shears cleanly before the SUPERWOOD substrate fails, the force required to break the adhesive ($\mathbf{F_{\text{glue}}}$) must induce a combined stress in the SUPERWOOD (membrane tension plus eccentricity bending) that stays safely below SUPERWOOD's 150 MPa Modulus of Rupture (MOR). MOR is a flexural strength; it is the appropriate substrate limit here because the offset joint loads each adherend in combined tension and bending.

The peak substrate stress follows from the rigid-adherend approximation for a single-lap joint under axial tension (bending-moment factor $k \approx 1$, so the bending term is three times the membrane term):

$$\sigma_{\text{wood}} = \frac{4 \cdot F_{\text{glue}}}{w \cdot t}$$

Where $w$ is specimen width, $t$ is substrate thickness, and adhesive failure load $F_{\text{glue}} = \tau_{\text{glue}} \cdot w \cdot L$.

To maintain $\sigma_{\text{wood}}$ safely below 150 MPa while generating sufficient force to shear the 15 MPa adhesive, use these dimensions:

Why 6 mm thickness?

Substrate stress scales as $L/t$, so thickness directly buys overlap headroom: $L_{\text{max}} = \text{MOR} \cdot t \,/\, 4\tau$ doubles if $t$ doubles. 6 mm keeps the wood at 80 MPa under the 2.4 kN failure load, gives the coupon enough bending stiffness to resist out-of-plane peel, and lets grip shims be cut from the same sheet stock so they cannot compress differently than the specimen.

Why 20 mm width?

Width cancels out of substrate stress ($\sigma_{\text{wood}} = 4\tau L/t$), so it cannot change which part fails first — it is chosen on practical grounds. 20 mm fits standard 25 mm Instron wedge grips, keeps edge defects and squeeze-out fillets a small fraction of bond area, and scales the failure force to about half of a 5 kN load cell.

Why 8 mm overlap?

Overlap is the one lever that decides which part fails first: substrate stress is $4\tau L/t$, giving $L_{\text{max}} = 15$ mm at these defaults. 8 mm holds a $1.9\times$ margin so the glue always breaks before the wood, while staying long enough that a $\pm 0.5$ mm bonding-jig error is only ~6% of bond area (it would be $\pm 10\%$ at 5 mm). Longer overlaps also concentrate shear at the overlap ends, understating adhesive strength.

Why 100 mm coupons?

Each coupon needs enough length for the wedge grips to clamp fully at one end plus free length between grip and joint so grip stresses die out before the bondline. 100 mm covers both with margin and is a convenient standard cut; two coupons minus the 8 mm overlap give the 192 mm assembly.

Why MOR 150 MPa?

150 MPa is the modulus of rupture for SUPERWOOD. MOR — a flexural strength — is the right substrate limit because the offset joint loads each adherend in combined tension and bending, which is closer to a flexure test than a pure tension test. If your batch data differs, adjust it here: $L_{\text{max}}$ scales linearly with MOR.

Why glue strength 15 MPa?

15 MPa is the expected shear strength of the adhesive under test — the quantity the experiment measures. It sets the design point, not a result: enter your adhesive's datasheet value and check the badge, since $L_{\text{max}}$ shrinks inversely with $\tau$ (a 30 MPa adhesive halves it to 7.5 mm).

2. Preparing the Instron Machine Setup

Because single-overlap coupons have offset centerline axes, direct mounting in tension grips causes severe diagonal twisting under load, generating out-of-plane peel stress that fractures wood prematurely.

To eliminate eccentricity, insert 6 mm thick shims (spacer blocks) in the upper and lower grips. Using scrap pieces of your 6 mm SUPERWOOD guarantees the shims will not compress under wedge clamping pressures ($E \approx 10\text{ GPa}$).

Step 1: Bond Specimens under Perpendicular Pressure Set the 8.0 mm overlap with a hard end-stop

Apply adhesive evenly over the $20\text{ mm} \times 8\text{ mm}$ overlap. Set the overlap with a machined end-stop or spacer in the bonding jig rather than by eye — at this scale a $\pm 0.5\text{ mm}$ error is ~6% of bond area. Secure under specified curing pressure until full polymerization, then after cure measure each specimen's actual overlap with calipers and record it: the strength calculation uses measured area, not nominal.

Step 2: Prepare Alignment Shims Must equal substrate thickness ($t = 6\text{ mm}$)

Cut two $20\text{ mm} \times 40\text{ mm}$ pieces from scrap SUPERWOOD. These shims offset the grip jaws by exactly 6 mm, centering the bondline along the loading axis.

Step 3: Load Upper Grip & Shim

Place top end of specimen into upper Instron grip. Insert a 6 mm shim on the opposite jaw face. Tighten jaw to secure specimen in a plumb vertical orientation.

Step 4: Zero Load Cell (Tare Weight)

Perform load cell zeroing in Instron Bluehill software to eliminate deadweight tare force of upper specimen grip hardware.

Step 5: Load Lower Grip & Shim

Insert lower specimen end into bottom grip with second 6 mm shim on the opposite jaw face. Verify alignment with a bubble level before locking jaws.

3. Executing the Test Protocol

The protocol follows ASTM D1002 conventions for single-lap shear; ASTM D2339 is the corresponding standard for wood substrates and governs the failure-mode inspection below.

  1. Quasi-Static Displacement Rate: Set crosshead speed to 1.27 mm/min ($0.05\text{ in/min}$) per ASTM D1002. This quasi-static rate avoids strain-rate effects in both adhesive and substrate.
  2. Data Acquisition Rate: Sample Force (N) and Crosshead Extension (mm) at a minimum frequency of 50 Hz.
  3. Shear Strength Calculation: Compute ultimate apparent shear strength ($\tau_{\text{app}}$) by dividing peak load ($F_{\text{peak}}$) by the bond area measured on that specimen after cure ($A = w \cdot L_{\text{measured}}$), so jig tolerance becomes bookkeeping rather than scatter: $$\tau_{\text{app}} = \frac{F_{\text{peak}}}{w \cdot L}$$
Failure Mode Classification (Post-Test Inspection):
Cohesive Wood Failure
Substrate wood fibers tear out before adhesive fails. Indicates adhesive bond strength exceeds substrate shear capability.
Adhesive (Interfacial) Failure
Clean detachment at the wood-adhesive interface. Points to inadequate surface preparation or contamination.
Cohesive Adhesive Failure
Adhesive splits down its center, leaving residue on both wood coupons. Represents true cohesive shear strength of the polymer.

4. Design Logic & Analytical Mechanics

Single lap joints carry a combination of stresses: axial shear $\tau_{xy}$, transverse peel $\sigma_y$, and an eccentric bending moment $M_0$. This section shows the substitution behind the dimension choices.

Why width cancels out

It is tempting to assume that reducing specimen width ($w$) allows a longer overlap ($L$). But $w$ cancels out of the substrate normal stress entirely.

Breakdown of equations:

  • Glue shear failure force: $F = \tau_{\text{glue}} \cdot w \cdot L$
  • Peak substrate tensile bending stress: $\sigma_{\text{wood}} = \frac{4 \cdot F}{w \cdot t}$

Substituting force $F$ into stress equation:

$$\sigma_{\text{wood}} = \frac{4 \cdot (\tau_{\text{glue}} \cdot w \cdot L)}{w \cdot t} \implies \sigma_{\text{wood}} = \frac{4 \cdot \tau_{\text{glue}} \cdot L}{t}$$

Key insight: substrate bending stress depends solely on the ratio of overlap length to substrate thickness ($\frac{L}{t}$). Width ($w$) only scales the total machine force required, not the internal stress.

Why 20.0 mm Width is Optimal

  • Edge Effect Isolation: Narrower coupons ($< 10\text{ mm}$) amplify edge defects and glue squeeze-out fillet artifacts, causing data scatter. 20 mm yields a uniform center stress field.
  • Load Cell Optimization: A $20\text{ mm} \times 8\text{ mm}$ overlap at $15\text{ MPa}$ shear yields $2.4\text{ kN}$ load—about $48\%$ of a standard $5.0\text{ kN}$ Instron load cell, comfortably within its accurate range.
  • Overlap Precision: Width cannot ease the overlap constraint — $w$ cancels from substrate stress — so overlap length is chosen directly for fabrication tolerance. At $8\text{ mm}$, a $\pm 0.5\text{ mm}$ jig error is ~6% of bond area (vs. ±10% at $5\text{ mm}$), while substrate stress stays at $80\text{ MPa}$, a $1.9\times$ margin under MOR.
